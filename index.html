01 / Community need

Why this project?

Focused on young people in and around Werribee.

🌊

Water is part of the community

Young people may spend time at pools, beaches and other waterways, making practical water-safety knowledge important.

🎓

Education can reduce risk

Simple, repeated lessons can help young people recognise hazards and make safer decisions around water.

🤝

Access matters

A free online resource could complement existing lessons for families who find regular swimming programs difficult to access.

02 / Global context

Identities & Relationships

03 / Proposed solution

A progressive learning pathway

LEVEL 01

Water Awareness

Recognise hazards, understand basic rules and learn how to make safer choices near pools and beaches.

LEVEL 02

Survival Basics

Introduce concepts such as floating, staying calm and getting help from a safe location.

LEVEL 03

Helping Others

Learn the importance of alerting an adult or emergency services and why people should not put themselves in danger trying to rescue someone.

Research

Find out what young people already know about water safety and what information they need.

Design

Create short, clear online lessons with visuals, activities and quizzes.

Test

Ask students and community members for feedback on clarity, usefulness and accessibility.

Improve & Share

Refine the program and promote it through appropriate school and community channels.

05 / Project analysis

SWOT

Strengths

  • Schools and community groups can promote the program.
  • Local pools and recreation facilities already exist.
  • Young people can engage with digital content.
  • Water safety is already recognised as important in Australia.

Weaknesses

  • Some learners may lack basic swimming skills.
  • Fear of water can affect participation.
  • Online education cannot replace supervised practical swimming instruction.
  • Some families may have limited access to facilities.

Opportunities

  • Partner with swim teachers or aquatic centres.
  • Run workshops during summer.
  • Use social media to spread awareness.
  • Create different levels of learning for different abilities.

Threats

  • Unsafe behaviour around water.
  • Low attendance or engagement.
  • Limited funding or equipment.
  • Bad weather or restricted access to facilities.
